## Cross-Cutting Patterns

**Pattern A — Trust collapses gradually.** It erodes across signatures-without-explanation → bill received → tests not justified → discharge friction → claim rejection → documentation runaround. BillShield enters at the post-trust moment.

**Pattern B — Pain threshold matters more than dollar threshold.** Patient D's ₹18K bill was clean and they didn't audit. Patient C's ₹25K bill triggered intense suspicion. The trigger is felt indignity, not the amount.

**Pattern C — The "ask the family" pattern is universal.** Three of five patients mentioned a family member or HR helping. BillShield positions as the second opinion that doesn't have a conflict of interest.

**Pattern D — Hindsight regret, not real-time action.** All five interviews surface as "looking back" stories. This validates Q1's Meera-not-Ankit decision.

**Pattern E — The system-promise gap.** The insurance industry promises frictionless cashless treatment; the lived reality is a documentation runaround that outlasts the hospitalization. BillShield's positioning: "the tool that holds insurers and hospitals to the rules they wrote themselves."
